## quick/ - small, fast-printing pieces to stress tools against (2026-09-21)

Thirteen STLs and `quick.json`, regenerated and byte-diffed by
`npm run quick:fixtures` (`tools/nso_quick_fixtures.js`); `npm run quick:test`
is the gate on what the set *is*, reading the committed bytes rather than
rebuilding them.

**This set is the opposite of everything else in here, deliberately.** The
other fixtures are validation fixtures: the Thingi10K-style meshes under
`repair/` and `open/` that are defective on purpose, the one-extrusion-wide
traced lattices, the raster inputs. Each exists to be a hard case, and most are
thousands of triangles of somebody else's geometry.

Every piece here is instead **small** (20 mm on its longest side at most),
**cheap** (12 triangles for the boxes, about 2k for the round ones - 8584 for
the whole set), **real** (generated by the shipped `NSO_Stock`, closed, and put
through the same canonical checker as everything else) and **quick to print** -
which is measured, not claimed.

So it is a set you can run a tool over without waiting, *and* a set you can
actually print to look at what the tool did. A 40k-triangle defective scan
cannot do the second half.

### The print-time figure is a model, and the model is written down

```
extruded length = volume / (layer height x line width)
seconds         = length / speed + layers x layer-change overhead
```

at 0.2 mm layers, a 0.42 mm line, 50 mm/s and 1.5 s a layer - a **100 per cent
infill solid**, the worst case, which a real slicer always beats because nobody
prints a test coupon solid. It is a proxy and it says so; what it is good for
is ranking and budgeting. The volume in it is the mesh's own signed volume,
measured off the soup about to be written. The budget is **ten minutes a
piece**, and the generator refuses to write a piece that misses it.

### What is in it, and why

**Five plain boxes**, twelve triangles each. The cube ladder is 6 / 9 / 12 mm -
roughly three times the material at each step, so a tool whose cost scales with
volume shows it across three points rather than two. A cube is the workhorse of
half the drive checks in this repo (Soften's square split, Seat's box-on-box,
Cut's lid, every paint test) and the only one there was is `box-20mm.stl`. The
plate and the bar are here because a good deal of code branches on aspect ratio
and a cube exercises none of it: one piece with a dominant flat face, one with
a dominant long axis.

**The eight stock primitives, all sized from ONE target** - a 10 mm piece with
a 1 mm wall, needing 469.668 mm3 at the default 30 per cent waste margin. That
is not decoration: `nso_stock.js`'s central claim is that a target's material
figure does not depend on the shape it is made into, so eight pieces sized from
one target hold the same material and therefore print in the same time. **The
set is a ladder in SHAPE with the time held still**, which is exactly what you
want when the thing under test is a tool and not a size. Built at 32 segments
rather than the card's 96, so a sphere is 1984 triangles instead of 18240.

The `ellipsoid` is the one piece asked for with an explicit ratio (1.6). At the
target's own h/d of 1 it would have come out **byte-identical** to the sphere -
which is `nso_stock.js`'s own assertion and worthless as a fixture.

Everything **rests on z = 0** and is centred on x and y, which is what
`NSO_Stock` produces and what a bed is. `fixtures/box-20mm.stl` is
origin-centred instead; it predates this and stays as it is, because
`tools/sculpt_selftest.js` and the Soften gate measure against that placement.

All thirteen are also real catalog rows, in the nest-library repo - the eight
primitives under **Stock Blanks**, the five boxes under **Test Fixtures**.
`tools/nso_quick_test.js` section 6 byte-compares every row against its fixture
here (given a nest-library checkout - see `library/README.md`), so the two
copies cannot drift apart.

## Hinge and strap fixtures: real boolean unions since 2026-09-16

`hinge_knuckle_box.stl`, `hinge_knuckle_lid.stl`, `hinge_pip.stl` and
`lid_strap.stl` (identical copies in nest-library; `hinge_knuckle_box.stl`
also in `library/`) were first written by an
early generator that concatenated closed shells with deliberate overlap. That
slices correctly (a slicer union-fills the overlap) but every overlap was a
real piercing pair, so Join / Subtract could not be run on them cleanly.

They are now the real boolean union of those shells, produced by
`tools/nso_fixture_union.mjs` through the same Manifold kernel `app-join.js`
ships. Measured with `tools/mesh_validate.py` (0 piercing, 0 coplanar, 0 open,
0 non-manifold on every one):

| file | tris before → after | shells → bodies | volume before (double-counted) → after |
|------|--------------------|-----------------|-----------------------------------------|
| hinge_knuckle_box | 780 → 730 | 4 → 1 (genus 3) | 741.084 → 636.096 mm³ |
| hinge_knuckle_lid | 524 → 460 | 3 → 1 (genus 2) | 526.056 → 456.064 mm³ |
| hinge_pip | 1468 → 1528 | 11 → 6 (5 knuckle units + the free pin) | 1295.343 → 1235.684 mm³ |
| lid_strap | 24 → 28 | 2 → 1 | 6120.000 → 6048.000 mm³ |

The "after" volume equals sum-of-shells minus pairwise overlap on each file,
so nothing overlapped three deep and nothing was lost. Bounding boxes are
unchanged and cross-section areas at five heights match the union-filled
originals to 3e-7 mm², so they slice exactly as before. `hinge_pip` stays a
multi-body file on purpose: the pin has clearance inside the knuckles.

`library/box_closed.stl` is NOT regenerated; see `library/README.md`.

## raster-real/ — real images the tracer did not draw (2026-09-19)

Six pictures rasterised by **Chromium's own Canvas 2D**, not by anything in this
repo, written by `tools/nso_raster_real_fixtures.mjs`: antialiased, at 23°, 67°
and 113°, as real PNG and one real JPEG, with a hand-drawn wobbly stroke on
off-white paper among them. `ground-truth.json` records what each one really
contains — taken from where the endpoints were put when it was drawn, never from
anything the tracer reported.

They are committed as fixed artefacts and **not** byte-diff gated: Skia's
antialiasing is not promised to be stable across Chromium versions and a
byte-diff on it would fail for the wrong reason. The generator is kept for
provenance. `npm run raster:real` measures them in Node against the ground truth
and against an independent pixel probe (`tools/nso_raster_image_probe.js`);
`npm run raster:real-drive` opens two of them through the shipped file input in
real Chromium, which is the only place the JPEG is decoded.

`skew-cross.png` is the picture that found the crossing-bridge bug — see
`docs/RASTER-LINES.md`.

## raster-lines/ — the flat-printed-line importer's inputs and outputs (2026-09-18)

Four rasters and the STL each one builds, regenerated and byte-diffed by
`npm run raster:fixtures` (`tools/nso_raster_fixtures.js`); `raster-lines.json`
carries every number. These are **inputs** as well as outputs: the `.pbm` /
`.pgm` files are what `nso_raster_lines.js` is fed, the `.stl` files are what it
builds from them.

Netpbm rather than PNG here on purpose — uncompressed, stdlib-only to read, and
the one raster format a text editor can still open. (PNG *is* readable now, by
`tools/nso_png.js`; these predate it and stay as they are, because their whole
value is being byte-reviewable in a diff.) The binary ones are P4 (packed
1-bit, 99 KB for 900²); `zigzag-layer-aa.pgm` is P5 grayscale because a
threshold is exactly what it exists to exercise.

**Every one is drawn with its lines at 45° to the canvas edges**, which is where
a slicer prints an interface layer. The first committed set was drawn
axis-aligned — measured at 94.03% of its wall length in the 89–91° band and
0.00% at 45° — and nothing caught it, because every other number in the suite is
invariant under a rigid turn. `npm run raster:angle` is the instrument that does
catch it, `raster-lines.json` records each fixture's measured angle, and the
canvas is 45 mm because a 30 mm pattern turned 45° needs 30·√2 = 42.43 mm.

| file | what it is |
|------|------------|
| `zigzag-layer.pbm` | the reference: one layer of the real two-layer zigzag, 15 lines at 2.0 mm pitch over 30 mm, at 45°, drawn with a 0.25 mm stroke |
| `zigzag-layer-fat.pbm` | the same centrelines drawn 0.65 mm wide, 2.3× the ink — must trace to the same line |
| `zigzag-layer-aa.pgm` | the same centrelines, anti-aliased, at half the resolution |
| `crosshatch.pbm` | 15 lines each way in one picture — 225 four-way junctions, at 45° and 135° |

Every STL: 0 open, 0 non-manifold, 0 degenerate, 0 sliver, 0 piercing, 0
coplanar, exactly 0.300 mm tall, volume = top-face area × height. The three
zigzag variants trace within 0.8% of the same 469 mm of line — not the same
triangle count any more, which was an axis-aligned artefact: Zhang–Suen chamfers
a diagonal corner more heavily the fatter the stroke. `crosshatch.stl` is Euler
−390 = genus 196 = the 14 × 14 holes in the lattice. See `docs/RASTER-LINES.md`.

`crosshatch.pbm` is a picture of a *finished* interface seen from above, and it
is a **tracer** fixture, not a printable layer: a layer with lines both ways
cannot be crossed by the layer above it. What a printable crosshatch layer looks
like is in `crosshatch/` below.

## crosshatch/ — the crosshatch as two one-direction layers (2026-09-18)

Four STLs and `crosshatch.json`, regenerated and byte-diffed by
`npm run crosshatch:fixtures` (`tools/nso_crosshatch_fixtures.js`). Built by
`nso_crosshatch.js` at the shipped crosshatch relief's **own** numbers — 1.2 mm
pitch, a 0.42 mm line, 0.3 mm per layer — so these and the shipped loose patch
the audit measures are the same pattern differing in the construction alone.

| file | what it is |
|------|------------|
| `layer1.stl` / `layer2.stl` | one printed layer on its own: a boustrophedon at 45° / 135°, 972 tris, closed, Euler 2, 0 piercing |
| `interface.stl` | the two of them, layer 2 set down on layer 1 — 1944 tris, two shells touching at z = 0.3, Euler 4 |
| `interface-45.stl` | **the re-tile**: a 45 mm target instead of 30. Same pitch, same line, same channel, 37 repeats instead of 25, and the first 25 line centres identical bit for bit |

The number they exist to hold: **110.2500 mm² of contact in 625 isolated patches,
every one exactly 0.176400 mm², none spanning more than one line width's
diagonal** — against the bidirectional lattice's 519.750 mm² in a single patch
across the whole footprint. `crosshatch.json` records both sizes' contact,
per-layer angle histograms and line centres. See `docs/CROSSHATCH.md`.
